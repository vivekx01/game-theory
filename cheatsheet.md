# Game Theory — Cheat Sheet

One line + one trigger question per concept. Skim this before a real
negotiation, disagreement, or decision — that's the point of it.

| Concept | One-liner | Ask yourself |
|---|---|---|
| **Dominant strategy** (Prisoner's Dilemma) | Individually rational choices can trap a whole group in a worse outcome, purely from lack of trust. | Is this a trust problem, not a stupidity problem? Can I fix the incentives instead of asking people to "be nicer"? |
| **Nash Equilibrium** | A stable point where nobody wants to change their move alone — not necessarily a good outcome, just a stuck one. | If I change my move here, will others just adapt and cancel out my gain? Am I fighting an equilibrium? |
| **Battle of the Sexes** (multiple equilibria) | Sometimes the fight isn't about who's right — there are multiple fine options and the real problem is picking one together. | Is this actually a logic disagreement, or just a coordination problem? Do we just need a convention? |
| **Matching Pennies** (mixed strategies) | In pure conflict with no stable fixed plan, being predictable gets you exploited. | Have I become predictable to someone who's adapting to me? Should I be deliberately, calculatedly unpredictable? |
| **Shadow of the future** (repeated games) | Cooperation becomes rational once you expect to deal with someone again — the future cost of broken trust outweighs today's one-time gain. | Is this a one-off interaction or a repeated one? Does the other side know whether we'll deal again? |
| **Tit-for-Tat vs. Grim Trigger** | How forgiving you should be depends on how *sure* you are it was real betrayal, not a mistake. | Is this clear, provable betrayal (cut ties for good) or an ambiguous/noisy signal (punish but stay open to repair)? |
| **Credible vs. empty threats** (sequential games) | A threat only matters if following through is actually in the threatener's own interest once the moment arrives. | Would they really prefer to follow through, or are they bluffing? If it's my threat — have I made backing down costly for myself? |
| **Signaling / adverse selection** | Cheap claims are worthless ("cheap talk") — only costly signals that are *cheaper for the genuine type* are believable. | Am I trusting a free claim I shouldn't? What would actually cost more for a fake version of this to produce? |
