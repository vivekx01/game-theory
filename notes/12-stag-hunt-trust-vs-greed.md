# 12 — The Stag Hunt: Trust vs. Greed as Different Diseases

## The setup

Two hunters can hunt **Stag** together (needs both committed, big payoff
if it works) or each hunt **Hare** alone (small but guaranteed payoff, no
cooperation needed). If one commits to Stag while the other bails for
Hare, the Stag hunter gets **nothing** — you can't catch a stag alone.

|                  | B: Stag | B: Hare |
|------------------|---------|---------|
| **A: Stag**      | 10, 10  | 0, 3    |
| **A: Hare**      | 3, 0    | 3, 3    |

## Two equilibria, but no greed problem

Checking both outcomes: at **(Stag, Stag)**, neither wants to switch
(10 beats 3). At **(Hare, Hare)**, neither wants to switch either
(3 beats 0). **Both are genuinely stable Nash Equilibria** — but
(Stag, Stag) is strictly better for both people.

Unlike the Prisoner's Dilemma, nobody here actually wants to betray
anyone — there's no profit in it. The problem isn't greed; it's **risk**.
If you commit to Stag and your partner flakes, you get the worst possible
outcome (0). Hare guarantees 3 regardless of what they do. So the moment
you're even slightly unsure the other person is fully committed, playing
it safe becomes the rational move — even though you'd both genuinely
prefer the cooperative outcome if you could just trust each other.

## Two different diseases that look the same from outside

- **Prisoner's Dilemma** ([[01-intro-prisoners-dilemma]]) — the problem
  is **greed**. Betraying is genuinely more profitable regardless of
  trust. Fix: change the incentives (contracts, enforcement, repeated
  dealings).
- **Stag Hunt** — the problem is **fear/uncertainty**, not greed. Nobody
  gains from the other flaking; everyone would prefer the good outcome.
  Fix: build *confidence/assurance* that the other person will actually
  commit — trust-building, not incentive-changing.

Misdiagnosing one as the other leads to the wrong fix: adding more
punishment/enforcement to a Stag Hunt problem doesn't help, because
nobody was tempted to cheat in the first place — what's missing is
confidence, not deterrence.

## Where this shows up in real life

- Startup co-founders half-committing because nobody's sure the others
  are "all in" — not because anyone wants to slack
- International disarmament that never happens even though every country
  would prefer mutual disarmament — fear of being the one who disarms
  first, not desire to attack
- Team projects where everyone under-invests because nobody's sure others
  will pull their weight, even though everyone would prefer everyone try
  hard
- Early-stage relationships/partnerships where both sides hold back,
  each waiting for the other to visibly commit first

**How to use this:** before trying to fix a stalled cooperation with
incentives, rules, or punishment, ask whether this is actually a Stag
Hunt, not a Prisoner's Dilemma — is anyone actually tempted to defect for
personal gain, or is everyone just unsure others will show up? If it's
the latter, the fix is a visible, credible commitment signal (a founder
publicly quitting their old job, going first on a small ask, allowing
outside verification) that reduces the uncertainty — not a bigger stick.
